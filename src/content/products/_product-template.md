steps:
  - Start with readiness and comfort. Read the opening guidance and complete the readiness profile. Consider your child’s communication, body awareness, bathroom access and the support your household can realistically provide. Address pain or other health concerns with a qualified professional.
  - Make the bathroom setup manageable. Prepare a stable potty or suitable toilet setup, supported feet, easy to manage clothing and handwashing supplies. Choose the simple words everyone will use.
  - Choose a small, repeatable routine. Select one or two predictable practice opportunities that fit your day. Use the bathroom sequence as a reminder without turning every few minutes into another potty prompt.

  - Give caregivers the same plan. Complete the shared family plan and childcare handoff. Agree on practical support and calm wording for accidents so your child encounters a familiar response.

Potty learning can become confusing when everyone is trying to help, but each person uses different words, different reminders and a different response to accidents.

One adult asks constantly. Another waits for a signal. Childcare has its own routine. Meanwhile, your child is learning several separate skills: noticing an urge, reaching the bathroom, managing clothes, sitting comfortably and asking for help.

The Potty Learning Consistency System brings those moving parts into one shared plan.

Its six stage pathway starts with readiness and comfort, then helps you build a routine, practise manageable skills and respond calmly when things do not go to plan. Eight printable tools turn the guidance into something caregivers can use together.

The aim is not a perfect week or a race to remove nappies. It is a clearer routine, less guesswork and a respectful approach that can adjust as your child learns.
