You prepare dinner, put it on the table and hear, “I don’t want that.” Again.

Perhaps your child wants the same few foods. Perhaps yesterday’s favourite is suddenly refused. You want to offer variety, but you are tired of guessing whether to encourage another bite, make something different or leave it alone.

Little Bites Mealtime System gives you a practical place to start.

The approach begins with what is already happening: which foods feel familiar, how they are served, when meals become difficult and what the adults say next. From there, you can plan one small change, offer opportunities to explore without demanding a bite and review what you notice.

The six-stage pathway—Notice, Map, Bridge, Explore, Repeat and Review—connects the explanations to tools you can use at home. Short scripts support everyday conversations, while the printable maps and trackers help turn “dinner was difficult” into clearer observations.

This is not about clean plates or judging your parenting by how many vegetables your child eats. It is about having a more consistent approach, noticing small changes and recognising when your family needs individual support.

The guide draws on public parent guidance from the [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx) and the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/). It is not endorsed by either organisation and does not replace professional care.
