You say, “It's bedtime.”

Suddenly your toddler needs water.

Then another hug.

Then a different teddy.

Then one more story.

Then they are hungry.

Then they remember something extremely important that apparently could not have been discussed at any point during the previous twelve hours.

Bedtime resistance can turn the end of the day into one long negotiation.

But before assuming your toddler simply “doesn't want to sleep,” look at the routine surrounding bedtime.

A predictable bedtime routine helps a toddler understand that the day is winding down and what is going to happen next.

## Why Toddlers Fight Bedtime

There is rarely one single reason.

Your toddler may:

- still feel energetic
- dislike stopping play
- want more time with you
- be testing boundaries
- be overtired
- have become used to delaying bedtime
- genuinely not know what happens next

The solution is therefore not always “make them more tired.”

Often, the bigger issue is predictability.

## 1. Create the Same Short Sequence Every Night

Your bedtime routine does not need ten steps.

In fact, complicated routines can become another source of negotiation.

A simple routine might be:

**Bath → pyjamas → brush teeth → book → cuddle → bed**

Choose a sequence that works for your household and repeat it.

Your toddler gradually begins learning:

_After this comes this._

That is the goal.

## 2. Start Winding Down Before You Expect Sleep

Moving directly from running, shouting, screens or rough play into bed asks your toddler to make a very abrupt transition.

Create a quieter period before bedtime.

Dim the environment.

Slow down the activities.

Choose books, cuddles or calm play rather than highly stimulating activities.

Bedtime begins before your toddler actually gets into bed.

## 3. Keep the Routine Predictable

If bedtime looks completely different every night, your toddler has more uncertainty to manage.

Predictability does not mean your family must operate like a military schedule.

It simply means the major steps remain familiar.

Your toddler should gradually know:

**“After my book, I go to bed.”**

Not:

**“After my book, maybe we play. Maybe I watch another video. Maybe Mum lets me stay downstairs. Maybe tonight I get three more stories.”**

Inconsistency creates room for negotiation.

## 4. Give Small Choices Inside the Boundary

You can offer some control without changing the bedtime boundary.

For example:

“Blue pyjamas or green pyjamas?”

“This book or that book?”

“Do you want Teddy or Bunny in bed?”

The choice belongs to your toddler.

The boundary does not.

Bedtime is still happening.

## 5. Stop Adding New Steps Because Your Toddler Protests

This is where bedtime routines often grow out of control.

One more drink becomes normal.

Then another story becomes normal.

Then lying beside the bed for thirty minutes becomes normal.

If every protest creates another step, your toddler learns that bedtime is negotiable.

Decide what genuinely belongs in the routine before bedtime begins.

Then keep it calm and consistent.

## 6. Use a Visual Bedtime Routine if Words Are Not Enough

Some toddlers respond better when they can see the sequence.

You might use simple pictures showing:

1. Bath
2. Pyjamas
3. Brush teeth
4. Book
5. Bed

Instead of repeatedly explaining the entire routine, you can point to what comes next.

This is especially useful for toddlers who struggle when one activity ends and another begins.

## 7. Give the Routine Time to Become Familiar

Do not judge a new bedtime routine after one difficult night.

Your toddler is learning a new pattern.

Consistency matters more than perfection.

The goal is not a child who instantly becomes delighted every time you say “bedtime.”

The goal is a child who increasingly understands what bedtime looks like and what happens next.
